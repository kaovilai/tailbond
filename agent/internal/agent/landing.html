Get tailbond

tailbond joins a device's connections (Wi-Fi, mobile data, Ethernet, a phone over USB) into one faster, steadier connection to {{.Server}}, your home server. Your Tailscale machines (like the NAS) stay reachable through it.

{{if .Windows}}

tailbond doesn't run on Windows (yet). Pick the device you want to set up below: an Android phone (scan the code with it), a Mac or a Linux laptop.

{{end}}
Android Mac iPhone Linux

{{if .OnAndroid}}On this phone{{else}}On an Android phone{{end}}

The app bonds the phone's Wi-Fi and mobile data. It can also lend the phone's mobile data to a travel router or a Mac.

Good to know before you install: while tailbond is on, it takes over from Tailscale on the phone (Android runs one VPN app at a time). Your Tailscale machines keep working, through {{.Server}}, and all the phone's internet travels through {{.Server}}. What pauses meanwhile: Tailscale's own extras on the phone, like exit nodes and Taildrop. Turn tailbond off and Tailscale is back as before. The app tells you when there's a new version.

3

Turn tailbond on

Once approved, tailbond shows what it will do: tap Add phone. Then tap the big switch at the top of tailbond. tailbond explains that it takes over from Tailscale, and Android asks once to allow its connection: tap OK.

Your Tailscale machines keep working: tailbond reaches them through {{.Server}}. Android runs one VPN app at a time, so Tailscale's own switch is off while tailbond is on; nothing is broken. The machines see the phone as {{.Server}}, so they allow it whatever they allow {{.Server}}.

All the phone's internet travels through {{.Server}} while tailbond is on (tailbond's Settings change that). To go back to Tailscale, tap Switch back to Tailscale in tailbond.

On a Mac

tailbond on a Mac bonds its Wi-Fi, an Ethernet or USB-C adapter, an iPhone plugged in by USB (Personal Hotspot on) and nearby phones' shared mobile data into one tunnel to {{.Server}}. While it's on, the Mac's internet travels through {{.Server}}; Tailscale stays on next to it, as before.

For Macs with Apple silicon: Apple menu → About This Mac → Chip says "Apple M…". A Mac with an Intel processor can't run tailbond yet.

What you'll see

tailbond's page on the Mac lists each connection by name: Wi-Fi, Ethernet or your adapter, iPhone (USB) when one is plugged in, and phones or computers that share their connection under Shared by nearby devices. Each can be Use it, Backup only or Off.

Have an Android phone too? Click Android at the top of this page.

Hotel Wi-Fi that keeps devices apart stops phones from sharing over it; an iPhone over USB still works. More: tailbond on a Mac (GitHub). To remove it: sudo /usr/local/lib/tailbond/uninstall.sh.

On an iPhone

Bonded with no app

An iPhone doesn't need anything installed to be part of the bond:

  • Join the travel router's Wi-Fi: everything on the router goes through tailbond.
  • Plug it into the travel router or a Mac running tailbond with a cable and turn on Personal Hotspot: its mobile data is bonded as iPhone (USB), and it charges meanwhile.

Keep using the Tailscale app on the iPhone itself: tailbond doesn't bond an iPhone's own connections.

tailbond Share: lend its mobile data over Wi-Fi

The tailbond app for iPhone is tailbond Share. It lends the iPhone's 5G/LTE to a travel router or a Mac on the same Wi-Fi, which bond it with their own connections (like Speedify's Pair & Share).

It isn't on the App Store. You build it once with Xcode on a Mac and a free Apple ID; a free signature lasts 7 days, so you rebuild it (or let SideStore or AltStore re-sign it) each week. While sharing, keep the app open on the screen.
