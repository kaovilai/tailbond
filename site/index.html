tailbond bonds Wi-Fi, 5G, ethernet and nearby phones into one fast, steady connection to your own home server or VPS. Your Tailscale machines stay reachable through it. No subscription, no third-party servers.

Get started View on GitHub

Early, open source (Apache-2.0). CI-tested everywhere; real-hardware testing so far is a GL.iNet Flint 3 home server.

How it works

A hub, which can be an Android phone or a travel router, opens one tunnel to your server over every connection it has, using Multipath QUIC (mqvpn). Your server combines them and sends traffic on to the internet and your tailnet.

  1. ConnectionsHotel Wi-Fi, 5G, ethernet, a phone tethered over USB, or a friend's phone sharing its data.
  2. HubThe Android app, or a GL.iNet travel router that bonds for every device on its Wi-Fi. A Mac hub is in progress.
  3. Bonded tunnelFaster together, or duplicated for calls that never drop. Fails over in seconds.
  4. Your serverA Flint 3 or Fedora box at home, or a VPS, with automatic failover between them.
  5. Internet + tailnetReach the NAS by its MagicDNS name, from anywhere.

Features

Everything a travel bonding service does, on hardware you own.

Four plain modes

Fastest, Smooth calls and video, Most reliable, Save mobile data. Per-link Always / Backup only / Off and data caps.

Tailscale-native

Pair a new device with one tap: it asks your server over Tailscale and you approve. Machines list, MagicDNS, setup health.

Home + VPS failover

Prefer home, switch to a VPS in about 15 s if home goes down, fail back when it's healthy.

Pair & Share

Nearby phones lend their mobile data to your hub over Wi-Fi: no hotspot setup, counted as app data.

Hotel Wi-Fi friendly

Sign-in pages are detected and kept out of the bond, and work with GL.iNet's Login Mode.

Bypass rules

Send a banking site, a work laptop or Wi-Fi calling direct, or through one chosen connection.

Native GL.iNet panel

Lives under VPN in GL's own admin, with GL's login. No reflashing.

Updates & backups

Releases are cut automatically. Routers and servers tell you when one is out, or install it themselves if you turn that on. Encrypted config backups and restore.

Get started

You need a server you control (a GL.iNet router, a Fedora box or a VPS) with a public IP or a forwarded UDP port.

  1. Set up the serverGL.iNet, Fedora or a VPS.
  2. Open its "Get tailbond" pageOn your Android phone, while Tailscale is on: http://<server>/. Travel router? Follow the GL.iNet guide instead.
  3. Install and ask to connectInstall the app and tap Connect; approve the request on your server.
  4. Add more linksPlug in a phone, share a friend's data, or bring the travel router.

Downloads are on GitHub Releases. Tips for the fastest setup: setup tips.

Questions

Is this a Tailscale product?

No. tailbond is an independent open-source project. It uses Tailscale (if you have it) for pairing, identity and reaching your tailnet; the bonded tunnel itself is mqvpn.

Can I keep using the Tailscale app?

On a phone, tailbond replaces the Tailscale VPN while it's on (Android allows one VPN at a time), and your tailnet stays reachable through your server.

Why my own server?

Your traffic goes to hardware you control, and it exits with your home IP. Speed is capped by your server's upload, so a VPS helps if home upload is slow.

Does it work on Heroku or Vercel?

No. A server needs inbound UDP and a TUN device. There are untested templates for any VPS, Docker and Fly.io.
